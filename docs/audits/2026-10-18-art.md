# Art audit, 2026-10-18 (Slow Horizons)

Report only. No generator code was changed. Everything below was measured on the current `gen/` (HEAD 510c786).

## Method

- **Sample:** 4000 random 32-bit seeds (Python `random.Random(20261018)`), drawn by an instrumented copy of `gen/` built in a scratch folder. The copy only adds a "which drawing step wrote this pixel" tag and a switch that makes one step's writes a no-op while the step still consumes the same random numbers. The first 3 pictures were byte-identical to `gen/gen_host raw`.
- **Visibility of claims:** each piece was drawn again with one element suppressed (sun/moon, milky way, aurora, stars, comet, shooting star, clouds, volcano smoke, lighthouse, sailboat, cabin, palms, glitter, fog, rain, lightning, snow, birds, glitch). The number of pixels that change is how visible the element is. Snowcaps were checked the same way (snowcaps forced off).
- **Contact sheets:** 24 random examples for every value of every trait (76 sheets), plus sheets for the rarest pieces, random samples, near duplicates and each defect. I looked at all of them; suspicious pieces were checked again at 4x and 6x.
- **Scans:** shade histogram per piece, far-ridge vs sky tone separation, cabin and lighthouse silhouette contrast against their surroundings, nearest-neighbour distance (8x8 block means) for duplicates, pair tables of traits for impossible or overweight combinations.
- **Scale check:** 12 pieces at 1x in the real DMG green (9bbc0f / 8bac0f / 306230 / 0f380f), 1x in their palette, and a 6x crop (`img/art/scale-compare.webp`).

Sheets are lossless WebP in `docs/audits/img/art/`. Seed labels on the sheets are hex. The legend after each label is time, sky, terrain, ground, weather, ridge count (`s` = snowcaps), extras (M milky way, S shooting star, C comet, B birds, L lighthouse, b sailboat, K cabin, G glitch) and palette.

## Defects, ranked

Counts are out of the 4000-seed sample (at a 2000-piece mint, halve them).

### Critical (looks broken or buggy)

**C1. The claimed low, striped or double sun is fully hidden behind the farthest ridge.** 65 pieces (1.6%; 4.4% of low-sky pieces, 44 of them have 6 ridges). The trait and the description ("A striped setting sun...") promise a sun that is not in the picture; only its glow is left. Two double-sun pieces lose both suns.
- Seeds: 6e402ffb, 5769fcbf, f9b1061d, 275b3265, 8b7a399d, 676dbba9, 6e25a30c, 1d549888, 74a689c9, 287ecf60.
- Code: `gen_sky.c` draw_light line 22 (`ly = top + rnd(2, 14)`) together with `gen_ridges.c` draw_layer line 185: the clearance only rescales a layer `if base > need`. With 5 or 6 ridges the farthest layer's base sits above the sun's centre, so it is never pushed down and its peaks cover the sun.
- Sheet: `img/art/hidden-sun.webp`.
- Fix: in draw_sky, cap the low sun like the high one: compute `base0` for every sky (not only `hi_obj`) and set `ly = imin(ly, base0 - 2)`. Or in draw_layer, when `base <= need`, lower that layer's whole profile until its highest point near `lx` sits at `ly`.

**C2. Volcano lava glow is a hard 7x5 white rectangle.** All volcanoes at dusk, night and aurora: 126 pieces (52% of volcanoes). At 1x it reads as a dead pixel or a UI block; at 6x it is an obvious rectangle sitting on the peak. When the moon is near it, the two collide (6fdb270e).
- Seeds: 07a6cb3d, 9dd44dc7, 2d3eb866, cc7be204, 6fdb270e, cdd0aff3.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_features line 45 (`setH(x, y, 0)` over a 7x5 box).
- Sheets: `terrain-volcano.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (07a6cb3d at 6x).
- Fix: replace the box with a 3 px wide crater line that follows the profile (`y = hgt[x]/8` for `|x - volx| <= 3`), shade 1, plus 1-2 shade-0 pixels in the middle. Let the existing soft glow (line 46) carry the light.

**C3. Volcano smoke by day is a solid dark "tadpole".** 116 daytime volcanoes (dawn, day, golden hour): the plume is 9 overlapping discs of one flat tone (150-182, shades 2/3) that get bigger as they rise. It reads as a black comma or slug, not smoke. It runs off the top edge in every volcano (242 of 242). It crosses the sun or moon in 42 of the 116, and because it is drawn after all ridges it can sit in front of nearer layers.
- Seeds: 62c5bbb9, 7aa546f7, f8a92510, 0b6fc043, d12c76dc, 4c1b92b6, fb096d11, 52d57bf1, 116655d6.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_features lines 36-43 (`g_puff = 150 + k*4`; radius `3 + k*13/10`; steps `px += rnd(2,4); py -= rnd(4,5)`).
- Sheets: `terrain-volcano.webp`, `time-day.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (62c5bbb9 at 6x).
- Fix: fade the plume. Make the tone go toward the local sky tone as k grows (`g_puff = sky + (150 - sky) * (9 - k) / 9`), keep the radius at or below 6, and stop the plume 6 px below the top edge. Skip puff pixels inside the sun or moon disc (the same `inlight` test the clouds use). Draw the smoke before the layers nearer than the volcano layer, or test `hgt` so it stays behind them.

### High (clearly ugly, or a dishonest trait)

**H1. Oasis palms are black scribbles.** All 236 oasis pieces (5.9%). Six fronds per palm droop with `j*j*11/100`, so they curl back into loops. Three or four palms overlap, and the waterline reflection mirrors them into closed "cages". At 1x they read as spiders or hairballs; at 6x as tangled wire.
- Seeds: 10752095, c90ccdd4, 1c1e1714, 5e3c7f3a, 0fb9e0f0, 8df94296, e0f0b785.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_features lines 84-103.
- Sheets: `ground-oasis.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (10752095).
- Fix: use 4-5 fronds with a gentle arc (droop `j*j*4/100`, length 7-10, a 2 px thick base), spacing of at least 14 px, and a clear trunk gap between palms. Stand the palms on a sand bank 2-3 px above the waterline so their reflection is separate from them. Or draw a fixed 2-3 frame hand-made palm sprite, mirrored and leaned at random.

**H2. Mesas are stacked flat rectangles.** 477 pieces (11.9%), the third most common terrain. `winmax` (max then min) turns the profile into flat-topped plateaus with vertical walls. Every layer does it, so a scene becomes boxes behind boxes. Far layers become pale slabs that fill half the sky (ebefe27f, aee60ba1, c2b9e8bb, 5019991a). There is no talus slope, so the walls look like Minecraft blocks.
- Seeds: c90ccdd4, aee60ba1, c2b9e8bb, 0777da6d, 1613e0ca, ebefe27f, c74203af, baa19cb7, 5019991a, 43a99551.
- Code: `gen_ridges.c` draw_layer lines 136-142 (`lv = 102 + rnd(0,51)`, `winmax(hs,4,1); winmax(hs,4,0)`, weight 218/38).
- Sheets: `terrain-mesas.webp`, `ground-oasis.webp`.
- Fix: draw mesas only on the 1-2 nearest layers and use soft peaks or hills for the far ones. Add a talus: after the winmax, blend the wall with a 6-10 px linear slope at the foot (`hs = max(hs, slope)`). Push `lv` higher (140-190) so plateaus are narrower buttes. Cap the plateau width at about 40 px by forcing a dip.

**H3. Cabins vanish in forests; only the smoke and the window show.** 68 cabins in forests (38% of cabins). 49 of the 180 cabins have a silhouette whose 2 px surroundings are at least 60% the same black shade. The cabin is black on the black near ridge and trees; the smoke (tone 51 by day) is a lone light diagonal streak that reads as a feather or a stray mark. The trait "cabin" and "a lit cabin" are not readable.
- Seeds: 121a8aac, 02d5fda1, b0923336, d3c45594, 97b06176, bbf1aca3, dae7214b, 097fc920, 4f63fc1a.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_features lines 72-83 (body `setH(...,3)`, smoke `g_puff = night ? 128 : 51`).
- Sheets: `cabin-invisible.webp`, `extra-cabin.webp`.
- Fix: on forest ground, clear a 20 px glade first (redraw the ground tone and drop the trees within ±10 px of `best`), or put the cabin on the second-nearest layer. Give the cabin a shade-1 roof edge and wall line on the lit side (1 px rim, like the ridges). Make the smoke 2 px wide and fade toward the sky tone, as in C3.

**H4. The gibbous moon is drawn as a bitten cookie.** All 200 gibbous moons (5%). The shadow is a full circle offset by 1.45 r, so the lit part has a concave bite (Pac-Man shape). A real gibbous moon has a convex elliptical terminator. With the crater dots it often reads as a face (e997aa09, 0277cf51). On aurora skies it breaks into an odd hourglass (121a8aac, 7cda4d78).
- Seeds: 3e8d4f7f, 79758f94, 7754127a, e02e3377, 79e62be8, 121a8aac.
- Code: `gen_core.c` fn_moon line 217 and moon line 231 (`g_off = rad*29/20` for phase 1).
- Sheets: `time-night.webp`, `sky-gibbous_moon.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (3e8d4f7f).
- Fix: phase 1 is lit if `x*side >= 0`, or else if `x*x*k2 + y*y*256 >= rad*rad*256` with an ellipse semi-axis of about 0.55 r (`k2 = 256*256/(55*55/100)`, scaled to stay in 16 bits). That is a lit half plus half an ellipse. Phase 3 (crescent) is also better as the lit half minus an ellipse, rather than two circles.

**H5. Lighthouses read as floating dashes in front of dark shores.** 137 of 209 lighthouses (66%) have their shade-3 stripes against shade 2-3 surroundings (lakes and fjords, where the near ridge comes down to the waterline). Only the white stripes remain: a dashed white line hanging over the water, and a dashed reflection under it.
- Seeds: 154425f0, cf6fecd7, 0f254311, d81fce16, d14c3481, 33ff8e59, 07143e65, 2b433148.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_features lines 52-64 (stripes `(y/3)%2 ? 0 : 3`, rock `lit ? 2 : 3`).
- Sheets: `extra-lighthouse.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (154425f0).
- Fix: when the background tone behind the tower is 128 or more, use shades 0 and 1 (or 0 and 2) for the stripes and add a 1 px shade-1 outline on the lit side. Or allow lighthouses only on islands and sea, where the sky behind them is light.

**H6. The farthest ridge dissolves into the sky and leaves floating light strokes.** About 125 pieces (3%) where the far layer is within 0.25 shade of the sky next to it. It is worst at golden hour (53 of 523, 10%) and eclipse (24 of 122). Usually it is only soft haze, but where the rim light still shows, the ridge becomes floating diagonal light slivers in the sky with no mountain under them. Dunes and islands (whose far layer is peaks) show it most.
- Seeds: 1848a039, b0ec8361, 7449bb7d, 2b27dbda, 51ad45b0, e20174e6, 39aab2eb.
- Code: `gen_ridges.c` draw_layer line 95 (`far = skyAt(skyBottom) + 48`), line 198 (mist up to 64 + 32 fog subtracted on layer 0), line 224 (rim light is applied regardless of body contrast).
- Sheets: `ghost-top.webp`, `scale-compare.webp` (1848a039 at 6x).
- Fix: clamp each layer's row tone to at least `skyAt(y) + 20` (`bv[y] = max(bv[y], skyAt(y) + 20)`), and scale the rim by the body contrast: `rim = min(rimA, 2 * (tl - mistrow[y] - skyAt(y0)))`, so a ridge that is not there has no rim.

**H7. Shooting star, comet and birds are often hidden or invisible.**
- Shooting star: 15 of 82 (18%) have fewer than 8 visible pixels and 7 have none. It is drawn in draw_light, before clouds and ridges, at `sy` up to 35% of the sky height, so ridges and clouds cover it.
- Comet: 11 of 75 have fewer than 25 visible pixels (the tail is `addT -90`, under clouds or ridges).
- Birds: 17 of 463 have fewer than 12 visible pixels: shade-3 birds over dark dusk skies or dark ridges. 133 of 463 (29%) have a bird on the sun disc, where two birds make a smiley face (d3c45594).
- Seeds: e02e3377, f5410400, 05034af6, b7cfc658, 79e62be8 (shooting star); f319907a, 78e95ebd, b3c14668, 665f6ed4 (birds); 5019991a, ad051842 (comet).
- Code: `gen_sky.c` draw_light lines 157-174; `gen_ground.c` draw_weather lines 199-211.
- Sheet: `hidden-extras.webp`.
- Fix: draw the shooting star and the comet head after the ridges (in draw_weather) and only in the part of the sky above `hgt` of every layer, or pick `sy` above the farthest ridge's minimum height. For birds: choose the shade per bird from the background (`getT < 128 ? 3 : 0`), keep the flock out of `lrad + 3` around the sun, and keep `fy` above the farthest ridge.

### Medium

**M1. Eclipse scenes are two-tone mud.** 30 of 122 eclipse pieces (25%) use fewer than 3 shades (shade 2 and 3 only); together with 19 nights, that is all of the 49 pieces under 3 shades. The corona is the best sky object in the set, but the landscape under it is mid-tone soup.
- Seeds: 8614d741, 4e1f8ef2, 92c0a558, 33d29056, 1f6de48c, 4c9c0bdf, 1d2e2b82.
- Code: `gen_sky.c` line 12 (`ST[T_ECLIPSE] = {192, 110}`) and `gen_ridges.c` line 95.
- Sheet: `time-eclipse.webp`.
- Fix: a real total eclipse has a sunset-coloured ring on the horizon. Make the eclipse horizon lighter (110 -> 50-60) in a band of the bottom 15% of the sky, keeping the top dark. The ridges then separate against it.

**M2. Overcast is only a strip; under it the sky is bright.** In all 728 overcast pieces (rain, snowfall, storm) the dark deck covers only the top 26-34% of the sky. Under it is the normal clear-sky gradient, so a storm by day has a brighter sky than many clear days, and "A heavy grey sky" reads as a band.
- Seeds: 733ad9b8, eda3f3f1, 58cac43b, 29db99d6, d7679515.
- Code: `gen_sky.c` draw_sky line 12 (no overcast entry) and draw_clouds lines 208-214.
- Sheet: `weather-storm.webp`.
- Fix: when `sky == S_OVERCAST`, use a flatter, darker gradient (day: skyTop 140, skyBot 70; storm darker still), with the deck as the darkest top part.

**M3. Fog at eclipse is white and glows.** 18 pieces. `fogT` is 32 for everything that is not night, so under a black sun the fog becomes a bright lens over the water.
- Seeds: b0902e52, c1c18618, a1c885fa.
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_weather line 148.
- Fix: `fogT = night || t->time == T_ECLIPSE ? 122 : t->time == T_DUSK ? 80 : 32`.

**M4. Dunes are one repeated sawtooth.** 382 pieces. Every layer is the same zigzag (`lam` 28-60, 72% rise and 28% fall), so dune scenes look alike: all of the 12 closest near-duplicate pairs are dunes. Hot rim light on the far layer adds the floating slivers from H6.
- Seeds: eab685d6 / f67bb280, 20607fac / fc848a2a, 7095525e / 812e938f.
- Code: `gen_ridges.c` draw_layer lines 143-150.
- Sheets: `terrain-dunes.webp`, `near-dups.webp`.
- Fix: vary `lam` per crest (re-pick at each crest), mix 2 wavelengths, round the windward side (smoothstep instead of a straight ramp), and give the far layers a lower amplitude (0.2) so the near dunes dominate.

**M5. Oasis is a full-width lake.** All oasis pieces put the horizon at 80% across the whole width, so "an oasis with palms" looks like a lake with palms on one side. Sailboats are allowed on it (b019a386, 02dd0b6c).
- Code: `gen_core.c` gen_draw line 275; gen_traits (`wet` includes oasis for X_SAILBOAT).
- Fix: draw the oasis as a pool 40-70 px wide around the palms (water only in that range), sand elsewhere, and leave the sailboat out on oasis.

**M6. Second twin moon or second sun can leave the frame.** The small body is placed at `lx ± (lrad + 9 or 14)` with no frame check: 4 of 39 twin moons and 11 of 75 double suns can be partly cut (33138149).
- Code: `gen_sky.c` lines 123 and 130.
- Fix: put the small body on the side with room (`side = lx < 80 ? 1 : -1` for the second body) or clamp `lx` to `[lrad + 20, 160 - lrad - 20]` for these skies.

**M7. The glitch block is often a flat pale rectangle.** The inverted box (8-24 x 10-40 px) often lands on a flat dark area and becomes a uniform light rectangle that looks like a sticker or a rendering bug (0bb74a2a, 48495ba5, 919ae022, 3dcf3428, ad051842). It also makes the rarest pieces the messiest (5019991a, e1325f57).
- Code: `gen_ground.c` draw_finish line 245.
- Sheet: `extra-glitch.webp`.
- Fix: invert only every other row of the block, or XOR shade with 1 instead of 3, or place the block where the row-shifted bands are, so it reads as signal noise.

**M8. The milky way is too faint for the most common extra.** It is 11.7% of pieces but at most -29 tone (under half a shade), so at 1x it mostly reads as a change in the dither texture. A few pieces show it clearly (8f4034b7, 7fc63915), many do not (909cdfa4, df04e35f, 2aee4d2a).
- Code: `gen_sky.c` line 56 (`-29 * df / w`).
- Sheet: `extra-milky_way.webp`.
- Fix: -45 to -50 at the core, and steer the angle to 20-45 degrees (not near horizontal) so it crosses the sky above the ridges.

**M9. Shades 0 and 1 are almost equal on a real DMG.** In the DMG green (9bbc0f vs 8bac0f) the far ridge vs sky, snowcaps and many clouds disappear, because they rely on shade 0/1 contrast. The strong silhouettes (islands, near ridges, eclipse, moons) read well at 1x on DMG. This matters for the "drawn by a real Game Boy" story when the piece is shown on the DMG itself.
- Sheet: `scale-compare.webp` (column 1).
- Fix: none required for the minted PNGs. If the DMG screen matters, keep the important separations at 1 vs 2 or 2 vs 3.

### Low

- **L1. Moon craters as square dots read as eyes or faces** on small moons (b011c757, 0277cf51, e997aa09 in Neon). Fix: craters only when `rad >= 11`, as shade-1 1x2 pixel shapes, at most 2. Code: `gen_core.c` line 211 (CR table).
- **L2. Night snowfields are the brightest thing in the picture** (ad60acba, 30a6dedf, a03d26b6): tone 19-77 at night pulls focus from the moon. Fix: at night add 60 to the snowfield tone (`gen_ground.c` line 21).
- **L3. Negative palette turns every sun into a black disc** (af2942c8, 49cf0d4c): the image reads as an eclipse while the trait says "sun". It is acceptable as a rare gimmick (0.45%), but leaving Negative out with eclipse and suns would keep the traits honest.
- **L4. Red Visor at night** is almost black and dark red (1197814a, 8c6ab700); its lightest colour (ff5a4a) is mid-red. Fix: leave Red Visor out with night and eclipse, or accept it as a mood palette.
- **L5. Cabin smoke and the volcano plume always lean to the upper right** (fixed `+2/+3` steps). Fix: lean by `side`.
- **L6. Snowcaps are faint specks on some pieces** (eb1f7366, 14d46c98, a16a1451). Measured: every snowcap piece has at least 60 changed pixels (median 700), so the trait is honest. Fix (optional): allow snow on the second-nearest layer too.
- **L7. Island scenes are very symmetric** (horizon near the middle plus a perfect mirror gives "lens" or "diamond" shapes, 24396780). Fix: more ripple offset in the reflection (`wob` amplitude) and a horizon range of 60-76% instead of 64-72%.

## Per-trait notes

- **Time.** Dawn, dusk and golden hour are the strongest. Day is clean but sometimes pale. Night is good since the lighter-horizon fix (only 19/1096 under 3 shades). Aurora curtains are lovely, but the moon on an aurora suffers from H4. Eclipse has the best focal object and the weakest ground (M1).
- **Sky.** Sun, haloed sun, striped sun and the eclipse corona are excellent at both 1x and 6x. Low suns cut by ridges are beautiful when the sun shows (C1 when it does not). Half and crescent moons are good; gibbous is wrong (H4). New moon reads as intended. Ringed planet and twin moons are good focal points (M6 aside). Overcast needs a darker sky (M2). The day moon is rare and fine.
- **Terrain.** Peaks, hills and fjord are the backbone and almost always good (the best 12 are mostly these). Islands are calm and consistent but alike (L7). Volcano has the best single silhouette (a clean cone, 41046038) and the worst details (C2, C3). Mesas need work (H2). Dunes are monotonous (M4).
- **Ground.** Lake reflections with ripples and the glitter path are excellent, among the best parts of the set. Forest silhouettes are strong (but hide cabins, H3). Meadow tufts are good. Snowfield is fine by day and too bright at night (L2). Desert texture works. Oasis needs H1 and M5.
- **Weather.** Fog is beautiful by day and dusk (M3 at eclipse). Rain and snow densities are right and readable; lightning reads well. Clouds are fine; 19 of 915 "clouds" pieces show under 30 cloud pixels (hidden by ridges); cutting the low clouds or placing them above the far ridge would fix it.
- **Ridges count.** 3 ridges gives the clearest pieces; 6 gives depth but causes most hidden low suns (C1) and most faint far layers (H6).
- **Snowcaps.** Honest (see L6); best on peaks and fjord.
- **Extras.** The sailboat is good. Lighthouse: H5. Cabin: H3. Birds: good by day, H7 at dusk and over the sun. Milky way: M8. Comet and shooting star: H7. Glitch: M7.
- **Palettes.** All 20 work on most scenes. Weakest: Red Visor at night (L4), Negative semantics (L3), Thermal and Pocket on low-contrast scenes (fog, eclipse) look grey and flat. Strongest: Lavender, Ember, Glacier, Sea Glass, Origin, Dune.
